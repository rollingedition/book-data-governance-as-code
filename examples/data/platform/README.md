# The platform's data

The data every example in this book governs: the warehouse of an online learning platform that sells professional
courses to adult learners, which the book calls the platform. Generated by `prep/generate.py` from fixed seeds; never
edit these files by hand. Run the script again, in the environment of `prep/requirements.txt`, and it writes the same
bytes.

**Source:** generated for this book. No real person is in it: every name, email, phone number, address, card number and
IBAN is invented by Faker, a library for fake data, from locale-specific patterns. Nothing in it reaches a real person
either:
- **Email addresses** are on the domains reserved for examples (RFC 2606): `example.com`, `example.net`, `example.org`
  and their subdomains.
- **IP addresses** are in the three ranges reserved for documentation (RFC 5737).
- **Names** are as a person gives them on sign-up, without the titles and degrees Faker adds ("Herr", "Dr.", "B.Sc.").

Phone numbers, card numbers and IBANs are random in a valid format, so one may match a number or an account in
service: never call, message, charge or pay them. Card numbers pass the Luhn checksum, as real ones do, so detectors treat them as real. About a third of the phone
numbers aren't valid for the learner's country, because Faker's formats don't always follow the numbering plan, and a
detector that checks validity misses them for that reason alone.

| File | Rows | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `learners.parquet` | 5,000 | Learners: name, email, phone, country, city, street address, date of birth, sign-up, marketing and AI-training consent (unknown for sign-ups before Jun 1, 2025), status, support region, and free-text `notes` |
| `instructors.parquet` | 50 | Instructors: name, email, country, payout IBAN |
| `courses.parquet` | 200 | Courses: title, category, instructor, price in USD, language, publication date |
| `enrollments.parquet` | 20,000 | Enrollments: learner, course, date, progress, completion |
| `payments.parquet` | 17,562 | Payments for paid courses: amount, currency, card brand, the full card number (a legacy column), status |
| `events.parquet` | 100,000 | Learning events: learner, course, type, time, IP address, user agent |
| `tickets.parquet` | 2,000 | Support tickets: learner, channel, support region, agent, status, and the free-text `body` |
| `reviews.parquet` | 3,000 | Course reviews: learner, course, rating, and the free-text `body` |
| `pii_spans.parquet` | 4,292 | Every piece of personal data placed in the free text of tickets and reviews: source, row, start and end offsets, entity type and text |

**The data's dates:** sign-ups from Jan 1, 2024; nothing after Sep 30, 2026. The book's "today" is Oct 1, 2026.

**Countries:** twelve, weighted as a global platform's learners might be: the US first, then India, the UK, Germany,
Brazil, Canada, France, Spain, Australia, Poland, Japan and Mexico. Names, phone numbers and addresses follow each
country's formats, and Japanese names are in Japanese script. A learner's support region comes from their country:
the Americas, EMEA or Asia-Pacific.

**On purpose:** some of what a governance team finds in a real warehouse is planted here, so the book can find it.
Payments keep full card numbers in a legacy column; about one learner in ten has `notes`, and some notes hold a phone
number or a second email address; and tickets mix personal data with order numbers, invoice numbers and dates that
only look like it. Tickets also hold the cases detectors find hardest: names typed in lowercase, initials, first names
that are also ordinary words (Will, Grace, Summer), and phone numbers typed without separators.

**A caution about the generated text:** every ticket and review is put together from a fixed set of 30 sentence
templates in `prep/generate.py`, and every date of birth in them is written in ISO format (`1985-03-12`). Faker's street
names are often surnames ("Kapadia Street"), and its cities are sometimes invented ("Lake Sara"). A measure on this text
tells you how a detector behaves on these shapes, not how it will do on yours.
